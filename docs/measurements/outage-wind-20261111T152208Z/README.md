`summary.md` is generated by `tools/outage-summary.py`; `samples.csv` is the raw series (one sample a
minute, long format: one row per edge and one for the hub, per phase).

## What the store kept, which is the part that is not obvious

The edge replayed every batch under its **original** timestamp. What the *store* kept is
series-dependent, measured with `count_over_time(...[120m] @ <restore time>)`:

| Series | Samples stored | Expected at 12/min | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| `energy_windfarm_power_real_watts` (fast-moving) | 658 | ~1 440 | **46 %** |
| `energy_windfarm_frequency_hertz` (slow-moving, deadbanded) | 300 | ~300 | **100 %** |
| `energy_turbine_power_real_watts` (6 series) | 467–618 each | ~1 440 | 32–43 % |

Prometheus logged **13** `msg="Out of order sample from remote write"` lines during the drain: it
rejects the replayed samples for a series that already has a *newer* sample — so the fast series lose
their late history and the slow ones (whose replayed samples arrived before anything new) do not. The
hub answered `200` throughout; the collector never knew. Findings #60 has the details, and the
lesson is that "an outage loses nothing" is a claim about the **pipeline**, measurable at the edge
(`enqueue_failed`, queue depth), and "the store kept it" is a **different measurement**, per series.

## Method, and where it deviates from the harness

- `tools/outage-measure.sh` ran the outage and sampled once a minute. The restore+drain phase was
  driven by hand at 120 minutes rather than by the harness's own 150-minute timer, because the
  harness was edited while it was running (never edit a bash script that is executing: bash re-reads
  from byte offsets). The drain rows were appended in the same format by the same sampling functions.
- The per-edge table therefore includes **six** edges — the harness's site list was extended to the
  highway hub after the run began, so only the drain phase has highway rows.
- The end-to-end arithmetic does **not** close (361 471 points sent by the edges, 459 150 accepted by
  the hub). Both are cumulative counters over windows that do not coincide (six edge counters, two of
  which were recreated during the run, against one aggregate hub counter), which is why the loss test
  is `enqueue_failed = 0` and `refused = 0` rather than a subtraction.
- Disk figures in `samples.csv` come from `docker system df -v` and are **apparent** size; the
  allocated figure in the table above is from `du` on the volume (findings #59).
